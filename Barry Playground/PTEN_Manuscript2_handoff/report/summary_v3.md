# What this document is

A short, figure-led account of the behavioural case, drawn from the full report
*Pten KO mosaicism and spatial accuracy: a behavioural interrogation* (version 3), which holds the
methods, the controls and every statistic quoted here. Figure numbers in this document are its
own; the report's numbering is given in square brackets.

**The claim.** Mosaic Pten deletion does not produce one deficit that worsens with dose. It
produces two phenotypes, and the axis separating them is reliance on the cue against reliance on
the place. Low load gives a mouse that is slow to take up the cue rule but knows where things are.
High load gives a mouse that takes the rule up readily and then cannot function without it.

| | Pten KO Lo (< 15% KO, n = 5) | Pten KO Hi (≥ 15% KO, n = 6) |
|---|---|---|
| Learning the cue→reward rule | Acquires it, least readily of the three — 21.2 vs 8.8 sessions (p < 0.001, no overlap with WT) | Acquires it more readily than Lo — 14.0, median 11.5, inside the WT range |
| Place preference with a cue present | Intact — +12.0 points (p = 0.016) | Absent — −4.2 (p = 0.58) |
| Speed through the reward window | Like WT | **16.4 vs 11.5 cm/s at hidden phase 10 (p < 0.001)** |
| Dwelling at the hidden goal | Pauses the most of any group | 59 s against 134 s per session (p = 0.006) |
| Searching the places that used to pay | Scales with the set, like WT | Does not — group × phase p = 0.003; cued p = 0.198 |
| What they do instead | Search hard, aim badly | Run — they speed up when the marker is removed (p = 0.001) |

# 1. The two phenotypes pull in opposite directions

A severity model predicts that Lo sits between WT and Hi on everything. It does not. Lo mice are
the slowest of all three groups to learn that the marker predicts reward — every Lo mouse is slower
than every WT mouse, 15 to 28 sessions against 7 to 13 — and they are the group that holds on to
the remembered place most strongly when a marker is available. Hi mice are the reverse: half reach
criterion inside the WT range, and as a group they show no latent place preference at all. The
mosaicism point is panel c: the two KO groups are not two doses of one thing, they are displaced
from WT in directions that are close to orthogonal.

Note what panel a does **not** say. Lo mice are not unable to learn the cue rule — all five
reach criterion — they take about two and a half times as long. It is a rate difference, and that
is what makes the dissociation work: the group that is slowest on the cued task is the group whose
place memory is intact.

# 2. Cue against place, at the first rotation

With the marker moved to a new place and visible, a mouse that has learned only the cue rule goes
to the marker. A mouse that also knows where the goal used to be visits the old place on the way.
WT and Lo mice do; Hi mice do not.

# 4. The decline is a response to the rotation, not to the days

Each mouse gets a slope across the four hidden probes, and the cued sessions give the control.
Hi mice lose 2.9 rewards per phase (p = 0.003) and gain 2.13 cm/s of target speed (p = 0.005);
Lo mice lose goal-zone time (p = 0.004) without losing rewards; WT mice are flat. In the cued
sessions nothing declines, in the same mice on the same days. The one significant cued slope runs
the other way: Lo mice are still improving (+0.071 rewards per entrance per phase, p < 0.001),
which is their slow acquisition finishing.

![](figsv3/fig04_change_per_phase.svg){width=6.9in}

# 5. Arrival: the clearest statement of the deficit

Panel c is the measure to lead with, because it uses *every* entry into the goal — chance
crossings as well as runs the mouse aimed at — so it cannot be explained by which runs qualify as
directed. At the trained location the three groups are indistinguishable through the reward window
(Hi 11.1 cm/s, WT 10.1, p = 0.18). At the last rotation the Hi mice are moving at **16.4 cm/s
against 11.5 (p = 0.0005)**: they reach the goal and pass through it. Put the marker back, same
phase, same mice, and the difference goes (p = 0.18).

# 6. The set to rule out

By the last rotation a mouse has four candidate places in its history: the goal it is being
rewarded at now, and the three goals of phases 7, 8 and 9. Scoring pauses by which kind of place
they fall at turns a session into the answer the mouse gives. This is the strongest behavioural
result in the report, and it is a group × phase interaction rather than a difference in level:
WT and Lo mice add to the list as it grows, Hi mice do not, and with the cue present they all
behave alike.

![](figsv3/fig17_former_goals.svg){width=5.6in}

*Figure 7. The set to rule out. Pause time at former goals per session, by phase, with the goal hidden (left) and cued (right). The number of former goals in play grows 0, 1, 2, 3 across the four phases. Faint points are mice, lines are group means ± SE; beneath each panel are the mean per-mouse slopes and the Mann-Whitney comparing those slopes between Hi and WT; numbers above a phase are Hi vs WT at that phase. Pauses at the wall are excluded by the pause definition throughout.*

[report Figure 10]

**This should make John Kubie happy.** The measure that separates the groups is not whether
the mouse got there but where it chose to stop, which is the variable the task was designed to
expose.

# 7. The same mice, with the cue switched back on

The strongest evidence in the report is a within-animal control, and it is worth seeing rather
than reading. Same animals, same phase, same arena; the only difference is whether the goal is
marked. Removing the cue makes WT mice pause **more**, because the goal now has to be remembered,
and makes Hi mice pause less.

# 8. Braking: the Whishaw test, and where it stops

Whishaw, Cassel and Jarrard (1995, *J Neurosci* 15:5779) treat deceleration on approach as the
sign that an animal expects the goal at a place; their fimbria-fornix rats decelerated at the same
distance from the vanished platform as controls, which is how they concluded those rats knew where
it was. Two things come out of applying that test here.

First, the *shape* of the approach is normal. With each run scaled to its own approach maximum,
the speed profile against distance from the goal does not differ between groups (permutation across
the whole profile with max-statistic correction, p = 0.53; within-animal hidden minus cued,
p = 0.83). The deficit is not in getting there or in aiming.

Second, whether a run contains a slowdown at all does differ, and so does what happens at the goal.

![](figsv3/nf4_arrival.svg){width=6.5in}

*Figure 9. Each pair of points is one mouse, cue present on the left; p beneath each pair is the paired t-test; above each panel is the Mann-Whitney on the change between Hi and WT. Diamonds mark IED+ mice.*

[report Figure 12]

**Where this logic has to stop.** Braking at *former* goals is identical in all three groups
(15.2, 15.8 and 14.7 events per 10 m, p = 0.93). Hi mice do pass through the old places and do slow
at them. The deficit is not a failure to generate candidates or to check them; it is a failure to
turn a check into a stay.

# 9. What they do instead

A reward count would record Hi mice as impaired at phase 10 (5.5 against 15.2 rewards, p = 0.004)
and stop there. When the cue is removed, WT mice slow down — the task has become harder — and Hi
mice speed up: −1.80 cm/s in WT and −1.13 in Lo against +1.69 in Hi (Hi vs WT p = 0.001). They
enter the goal zone as often as WT mice (43.3 against 48.8 entries, p = 0.77) but cover about 29%
more ground to do it, and they leave again before the 1.2 s that earns a pellet.

![](figsv/sm__fig_speed_mode.svg){width=6.5in}

*Figure 10. a: mean speed per probe session by phase, cued (dashed) and hidden (solid), mean ± SE. b: the same comparison within each mouse — speed in the hidden session minus speed in the cued session at the same phase. Negative means the mouse slows down when the marker is removed. Faint points are mice. c: goal-zone entries against rewards at hidden phase 10, one point per mouse, with reference lines at one reward per three and per six entries.*

[report Figure 13]

So the entries cannot be used to say they find the goal. On the narrow question — does the
mouse reach the place? — they do. On the question the task actually asks — does it know the place
is there? — the speed through the reward window, the dwelling and the search of the history all say
no, and all three recover when the cue is put back.

# 10. The circuit, in one paragraph

Hi KO mice start with about twice the WT theta-band current density in the lower blade, at GCL2 and
MML2 where the KO cells and their perforant-path synapses sit (4-layer mean p = 0.043). CNO
silences hM4D-expressing Pten KO granule neurons in slice — spikes per sweep fall by 75%, two of
five neurons stop firing at any current tested (95% CI on the effect −6.9 to −1.6 spikes per sweep,
though at n = 5 the paired test is p = 0.051) — and in vivo it removes most
of the theta excess in 6 of 7 Hi mice while leaving WT unchanged. The interictal discharges arise
from the same two layers: the IED's initiation current is a sink in GCL2 with a source in MML2, and
baseline theta CSD in those layers identifies the IED+ mice almost perfectly (AUC 0.97), an ability
it loses after CNO. The continuous current and the intermittent discharge are two outputs of one
lower-blade circuit.

# 11. What to be careful about

- **Six Hi mice and five Lo mice.** Every group statement rests on single-digit n. The
  within-animal cued control is the strongest design in the set precisely because it does not
  depend on between-group variance.
- **The example mice are typical, not proof.** The three mice in Figure 8 were picked by a stated
  rule before the figures were drawn — each is closest to its group median across five of the
  measures shown here — and the group panel beside each pair of maps carries the inference.
- **Thresholds are choices.** The braking criteria and the pause definition were set once and not
  adjusted afterwards, but their sensitivity has not been tested.
- **Braking distance is measured only on runs that braked**, so the groups that brake less often
  contribute a selected subset; the proportion braking is reported beside it for that reason.
- **Only one per-phase probe result survives correction** for the number of tests run in the full
  report.
- **What the deficit is called is still open.** A capacity limit on holding and testing a set of
  candidate places is consistent with working memory, with pattern separation, or with both, and
  these data cannot separate them.
