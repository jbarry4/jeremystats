THE HIPPOCAMPUS
0:16.0-0:20.9  The hippocampus helps us form memories.
             (Its name means “seahorse,” after its curled shape.)
0:21.2-0:26.3  It is also one of the brain's most seizure-prone circuits.

THE MODEL (card)
0:26.5-0:39.0  Mice with a KCNT1 variant
               1  They carry a change found in people with KCNT1 epilepsy.
                  (Kcnt1 Y777H in mice; KCNT1 Y796H in people)
               2  It makes a potassium channel overactive.
                  (The sodium-activated K+ channel KNa1.1, or Slack)
               3  That quiets inhibitory neurons, the brain's brakes.
               4  Mice with two copies have frequent seizures, many of them in sleep.
                  (Shore et al., Cell Reports 2020)

HOW WE LISTEN
0:39.5-0:47.8  To listen in, we lower a thin probe with 64 recording sites through the hippocampus.
             (A silicon probe, in an awake mouse)
0:48.6-0:56.3  Each site hears its own layer, from CA1 at the top down through the dentate gyrus.

CELLS AND LAYERS
0:56.9-1:00.7  Granule cells are the gatekeepers: they catch the signals that arrive from the cortex.
1:01.0-1:04.9  In the hilus, inhibitory cells are the brakes: they keep the granule cells from firing all at once.
             (KCNT1 variants weaken inhibitory cells like these.)
1:05.3-1:09.3  What gets through goes on to CA3, then CA1, and out of the hippocampus.
1:10.0-1:18.8  Each kind of cell keeps its body in its own layer, and our probe passes through them all.

1:23.1-1:30.0  Each line is the voltage at one depth of the probe; the colors show current flowing into the cells (red) and out of them (blue).
1:30.4-1:38.4  Then, at zero, the whole hippocampus erupts, and keeps seizing for about 40 seconds.
1:39.0-1:47.6  In the half-minute before, the circuit stumbles: small discharges come faster and faster.
             (Each flash of the layers is one of them, as recorded.)
1:48.2-1:57.0  Meanwhile a brake cell in the hilus fires harder and harder, nearly 60 times a second, right up to the start.
