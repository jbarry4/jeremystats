One Channel, Eight Gates

The trial run: a single CSC#.ncs from end to end, with a pass criterion at every step so a mistake surfaces in the beta rather than after 64 channels.

No loops, no cohort, no metadata, no IED detection. One channel, one recording, one output file — and eight checks that each say what "working" looks like and what a failure means.

IEDs stay in — recorded, not removed

FindIEDs.m is out. Your call and it's the right one for this circuit: the windows of interest carry none, and leaving transients in tells you something. The cheap insurance is to record what you're keeping instead of detecting it: MakeEpochs.m writes a maxAbs and nSharp column per epoch and drops nothing. If a single transient ever turns out to dominate an epoch, you can find it in the table and test the assumption later — without re-running anything.

The QC figure carries the other half of that: the raw trace is plotted directly under the MI-over-time trace, on the same time axis. A transient-driven spike in MI is then visible rather than silently averaged away.

The number that decides the full run

30 minutes × 64 channels is 32 hours per session single-threaded, or roughly 4 hours on 8 cores — which is exactly your current SLURM wall time, with nothing to spare. Two dials: drop to 50 surrogates (halves it, and a z-score built on 50 shifts has a standard error around 0.1 — plenty), or narrow the fast axis. Decide this in the beta, not when a job array times out at hour four.

The one new function

Why ModIndexFast.m has to exist

ModIndex_v2.m computes one modulation index with eighteen separate find() passes over the epoch. That is fine when you call it 1 887 times, which is what the old pipeline did. The beta run calls it 18.9 million times.

The change is small: compute which of the 18 bins each sample falls into once per (slow band, epoch), then accumulate every fast band's amplitude against that one index vector. Shifting for a surrogate becomes shifting the index vector, not re-binning.

Keep ModIndex_v2.m in the repo unchanged. It is the reference. Gate 4 is the assertion that the fast one agrees with it to machine precision on the same inputs, and that assertion should live in a test file you can re-run, not in a comment.

Before you start

Pick the right channel

The beta channel should be one where you already know the answer, so that a null result is unambiguous evidence of a bug rather than of biology.

  • A channel with obvious theta. Somewhere in CA1 str. lacunosum-moleculare or the hilus, from a session where the animal was moving. If theta is weak, gate 3 has nothing to show you.
  • From a recording you have already run through newFCSE.m. Gate 5 compares against that output, so it needs to exist.
  • A control animal, not IED+. Not because IEDs are a problem — you're keeping them — but because for the beta you want the simplest possible signal. Run an IED+ channel second, and compare.
  • Long enough. At least 10 minutes, so 300-ish epochs and the time course means something.

What "done" looks like

All eight gates pass, and you have three things on screen from one channel: MI over time at the peak cell, MI across the seventeen slow bands, and theta power against MI. If those three look like anything at all, the design works and the rest is loops. If they look like nothing, you've spent half an hour finding that out instead of a week.

Runtimes measured on synthetic data of the same size and sampling rate as a 30-minute PTEN channel, using the same eegfilt and modulation-index arithmetic as the pipeline. Treat the absolute numbers as order-of-magnitude for MATLAB; the ratios between them are the reliable part.
