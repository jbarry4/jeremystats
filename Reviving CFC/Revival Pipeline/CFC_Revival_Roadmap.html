CFC Revival Roadmap

From .ncs to the theta-power heat map: every stage, what already exists, what has to be written, and three things worth settling before any of it gets written.

What the plan changes

The thesis version computed one comodulogram per channel per recording, collapsed theta to a 4–12 Hz band, and fed session-level means into a GEE. It works, and it hides three things the plan wants back:

  • when the effect happens — averaging over a whole recording erases the time course
  • which theta frequency drives it — the 4–12 Hz collapse throws that axis away
  • whether theta power is doing the work — never measured alongside MI

The fix in the plan is to stop averaging: cut every recording into 6-second epochs, compute a full comodulogram per epoch, and carry theta power per epoch alongside it. Per channel you end up with an MI array of θ × γ × epoch and a theta-power array of θ × epoch. Those two feed the new headline figure — gamma on y, theta power on x, MI as colour, one panel per theta frequency, per channel.

The good news, before the caveats

Structurally this is a smaller change than it looks. The expensive part of the existing pipeline — filtering the whole recording into two banks and Hilberting them — is exactly what epoching needs, and it happens before any epoch boundary matters. Most of the new work is a loop, a reduction, and a better file format. The measurement core, ModIndex_v2.m, does not change at all.

Decide first

Three things to settle before writing code

1 · Filter the whole recording, then cut. Never the reverse.

This is the one that would be genuinely painful to discover later. eegfilt builds a filter about three cycles of the lower cutoff long, and filtfilt needs at least three times that many samples to run at all. At 3 255 Hz a 1 Hz phase band is a 9 767-tap filter needing roughly 9 seconds of data — more than a 6-second epoch contains. Even at 4 Hz you are using a filter about 0.75 s long, so the first and last second of any epoch is contaminated by whatever sat next to it.

So: filter and Hilbert the continuous trace once, then slice the resulting phase and amplitude series into epochs. The existing newFCSE.m already does the first half of that — it builds PhaseFreqTransformed and AmpFreqTransformed for the whole recording before any MI is computed. You are inserting a loop, not restructuring.

2 · Store data, not figures.

The current chain saves a .fig per channel and then rips the numbers back out of it with CFCFigRipper.m. With epochs that becomes untenable — you would be writing a figure per epoch per channel. Write one file per channel containing the arrays and the epoch table, and generate figures on demand from the long table.

Size is not a problem. 17 theta × 37 gamma × 100 epochs in single precision is about 250 kB per channel, so a 63-channel session is roughly 16 MB — smaller than the .fig files it replaces.

3 · Pick the grids now, and pick them honestly.

The plan needs theta 4–12 Hz on the phase axis, which is a much smaller sweep than the current 1:0.5:26. That is a real saving — but the amplitude bandwidth needs attention at the same time. eegfilt delivers a passband of roughly 0.30 × the centre frequency regardless of what the driver asks for, and an amplitude filter narrower than 2 × θ physically cannot carry the modulation. At 12 Hz theta that means nothing below about 80 Hz gamma will register, whatever is really there.

Setting ampBW = max(10, 2.5*fp) explicitly makes the whole grid comparable instead of only its upper part, and it makes the amplitude axis mean the same thing at every theta frequency — which matters a lot more now that theta frequency is an axis you intend to interpret.

Risks

Three things that will bite, with numbers

All three are measured on simulated data where the true answer is known, using the same eegfilt + ModIndex_v2 arithmetic as the pipeline. Scripts are in 05_explainer/.

Risk 1 · Short epochs put a slope on the theta axis that isn't real

MI is biased upward when you have less data, and a 6-second epoch holds 24 theta cycles at 4 Hz but 72 at 12 Hz. So the bias is three times worse at the bottom of the theta axis than at the top — on pure noise with no coupling anywhere.

What to do

Do not compare raw MI across theta frequencies at 6 s. Z-score every cell against surrogates built within that epoch — shift the amplitude series against the phase series a couple of hundred times and express MI in standard deviations above that epoch's own chance level. Tested on pure noise, that removes the theta-axis slope completely: raw MI runs 0.00139 → 0.00033 across 4–12 Hz, z runs −0.04 → −0.06.

Risk 2 · The theta-power → MI relationship is partly an artefact of measuring theta better

This is the headline analysis in the plan, so it is worth being careful about. A stronger theta rhythm gives a cleaner phase estimate, and a cleaner phase estimate detects more of whatever coupling was already there. Holding the true coupling depth fixed and varying only theta amplitude:

Read that carefully, because it cuts both ways. Surrogate z-scoring does not flatten this, and it shouldn't — with no coupling present, z stays at zero no matter how big theta is, so the effect is not spurious signal. It is a real change in how much of a real effect you can see. But it means a rising theta-power → MI curve is ambiguous between "coupling increased" and "we could suddenly measure the coupling that was always there."

What to do

Three cheap defences, in order of effort. First, plot the actual distribution of theta power across your epochs — the confound saturates above about unit amplitude, so if your epochs mostly sit in the flat part it barely applies. Second, run the same analysis on epochs matched for theta power across groups, so the comparison is between conditions at equal SNR. Third, generate the null curve: simulate at each observed theta power with coupling held constant, and plot that alongside the data as the "detectability only" expectation. The third one is maybe half a day and it turns an ambiguous figure into a clean one.

Risk 3 · Epochs are not independent observations, and IEDs are not noise

Two separate problems that both land in the statistics.

Pseudo-replication. Going from one number per channel to a hundred numbers per channel multiplies your n by a hundred without adding a hundred animals. Consecutive 6-second epochs are also correlated with each other. A GEE that treats them as independent will produce p-values that are not meaningful. Cluster at the animal level and use a working correlation structure that expects autocorrelation — or move to a mixed model with epoch nested in channel nested in session nested in animal.

IEDs. A sharp transient deposits energy in every amplitude band at once, at a consistent slow-band phase, which is exactly the pattern MI is built to detect. Simulated: background noise plus 1.5 transients per second, with no coupling of any kind in the signal, produces a comodulogram peaking at MI 0.0093 — twenty-one times the noise floor. Since your groups are literally IED+ and IED−, an unmasked comparison between them is confounded by construction, and epoching makes it worse rather than better, because a single discharge now dominates the epoch it lands in.

What to do

Mask before you epoch, and carry the IED count per epoch through to the long table so it can be a covariate or an exclusion criterion later. Also worth doing deliberately: run the analysis on IED-free epochs from IED+ animals. That separates "this animal has epileptiform activity" from "this six seconds contains a discharge", which is a question the epoched design can answer and the session-average design could not.

Order of work

A build order that keeps you testable

  1. Get one channel running end to end, locally, with no cluster. One .ncs, filter banks, epoch loop, MI array, tidy file. Everything else is scaling.
  2. Validate against the old pipeline. Average your per-epoch MI array back over all epochs and compare it to the whole-recording comodulogram newFCSE.m produces for the same channel. They will not be identical — different epoch bias — but the blob should be in the same place with a similar shape. If it isn't, stop.
  3. Add surrogate z-scoring and confirm on a shuffled channel that z sits at zero across the whole grid.
  4. Add theta power and the epoch table. Cheap once the loop exists.
  5. Wire in IED masking. Sanity check: MI should drop on IED+ channels, not rise.
  6. Then scale to the array job, and only then write the merge and the three output figures.

Steps 1–3 are where the real risk sits, and they need one channel and a laptop.
