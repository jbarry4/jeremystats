Channel-First CFC Pipeline

One function that takes a CSC#.ncs and returns everything about that channel. Sessions and cohorts are for loops around it. No feeder sheets anywhere.

Your shape is the right one, and it's better than what I drew last time. Three things it gets right: the unit of work is a channel, not a job array; scaling up is a loop, not an architecture; and metadata never touches the signal code. Everything below is your chart with the missing boxes filled in.

Three decisions now locked in

No metadata at load time. RunChannel.m takes a path and returns arrays. It does not know what animal it is, what condition, or what layer. Those get joined in Python at the results stage, from the folder tree. That means you can re-label a whole cohort without re-running a single channel.
Non-overlapping 6 s epochs. floor(recording / 6) of them, no sliding window.
MATLAB for signal, Python for results. The line is SaveChannel.m: everything before it is MATLAB and touches voltage, everything after is Python and touches tables.

QC

Three figures, one per level

Each panel is here because it catches a specific failure. A QC plot that can't fail isn't QC.

CSC1_qc.png — per channel

1
10 s of raw and cleaned trace, IEDs marked. Catches a dead or clipping channel, and a detector that is firing on noise or missing obvious events.
2
Power spectrum, log–log. Catches line noise surviving the notch, a wrong sampling rate, and the flat spectrum of a dead channel.
3
Epoch inventory strip across the whole recording — kept / dropped-IED / dropped-artifact. Catches the channel where you kept 12 epochs out of 300 and would otherwise never have noticed.
4
Mean comodulogram over kept epochs, fixed colour limits. This is the familiar view, and the one to compare against the old pipeline.
5
z-map computed on a phase-shuffled copy. Should be flat at zero everywhere. If it isn't, the surrogate loop is broken. This is the panel that will save you the most time.
6
Theta power over time, 4–12 Hz stacked, plus its histogram. Tells you whether there is theta at all, whether it drifts, and where the epochs sit on the detectability curve that Output C depends on.
7
MI and z over time at the peak cell. The time course the whole redesign exists to produce — look at it once per channel before trusting any average of it.

session_qc.png — per session

1
Depth × channel grid of mean comodulograms, one shared colour limit across the grid. Catches a layer inversion, a dead shank, and a channel that disagrees with its neighbours.
2
Percent of epochs kept, by depth. Catches IED-heavy channels before they reach the statistics.
3
Theta power by depth. Should peak where your laminar work says it does. A cheap sanity check that the channel map is right.
4
Peak MI and peak z by depth. The laminar profile, which is the result you actually care about at session level.
5
Flag list — clipping, dead, excessive IED, too few epochs. Text, not a plot.

cohort_qc.png — per cohort

1
Counts per group — animals, sessions, channels, epochs. This is the n you will report, and it should be looked at before any model runs.
2
Epoch retention by group. The IED confound check. If IED+ animals retain 40% of epochs and controls retain 95%, every downstream comparison is between different amounts of data.
3
Theta power distribution by group. The Output C confound check — a stronger theta gives a cleaner phase estimate and therefore a higher MI at identical true coupling. If the groups differ in theta power, some of the coupling difference is detectability.
4
Pooled z distribution by group. Is there anything here at all, before modelling?

Two notes

Things that will cost you a re-run if skipped

The two branches must cut epochs identically

Output C joins MI and theta power on (channel, epoch). If CFC.m drops the first epoch for filter edge effects and ThetaPower.m doesn't, every row is off by one and the figure will still render, looking plausible and wrong. That's why MakeEpochs.m is its own function called once, above both, and the epoch table is saved alongside the arrays rather than recomputed.

The optional shared theta bank

As drawn, CFC.m and ThetaPower.m both filter 4–12 Hz — the same seventeen bandpasses, computed twice. That's roughly a 30–40% overhead on the per-channel filter time. Two ways out, both keeping the functions independently callable:

  • Give CFC.m a second output: [cfc, thetaBank] = CFC(sig, ep, p), and let ThetaPower.m accept an optional pre-built bank — pow = ThetaPower(sig, ep, p, thetaBank). Called without it, it builds its own.
  • Or pull out a tiny ThetaBank.m that both call, and let MATLAB's own caching handle it.

Neither is worth doing on day one. Build them separate, measure, and only merge them if the runtime actually bothers you.

Order of work

Every step ends with something you can look at

  1. LoadCSC.m + QC panels 1–2. Before computing anything, you should be able to point at a channel and see its trace and spectrum.
  2. MakeEpochs.m + FindIEDs.m + panel 3. Now you can see which parts of a recording survive.
  3. CFC.m without surrogates + panel 4. Average your per-epoch MI back over all epochs and compare it to what newFCSE.m produces for the same channel. Not identical — different bias — but the blob should sit in the same place. If it doesn't, stop here.
  4. Add the surrogates + panel 5. The shuffled z-map must be flat. Do not go further until it is.
  5. ThetaPower.m + panel 6.
  6. SaveChannel.m + RunChannel.m. You now have the primitive, and everything after this is loops and tables.
  7. RunSession.m and RunCohort.m. An afternoon, including their QC figures.
  8. Python. collect.py → attach.py → figures → stats.

Steps 1–4 are the whole risk, and they need one channel and a laptop. Everything from 6 onward is bookkeeping.
